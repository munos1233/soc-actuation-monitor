One A2 case, an approval-level downgrade, used to sit at zero under
\emph{naive} as well as under \emph{full}. That zero was an instrument
bug, not a non-switchable check: the injection wrote an invalid
uppercase \texttt{L1}, which the action enum rejects in both arms, so
the cell could not produce a one. The case now writes a valid
\texttt{l1} and leaves the planner-written fingerprint column
untouched---level is not one of the eleven content segments, so a
column comparison sees no change. Under \emph{naive} the vendor records
a submit; under \emph{full} the bind gate refuses with
\texttt{approval\_level\_drift}. The cell is therefore a missing bind
conjunct that the instrument had been hiding, not an honest naive zero
and not a result of the four switches. Until the 57 are re-frozen, the
headline that still prints 47/57 is not a current measurement.

Building the remaining Suite~A cases and the six B2 injections did not
add a third numbered fail-open of the kind the opening of this section
reports. It found missing conjuncts on gates the 57 already named, one
instrument bug that had been hiding a conjunct, and a liveness path
that is not a safety hole. The distinction matters for what the tables
are allowed to claim.

The approval-level downgrade was the instrument bug
(\S\ref{sec:eval-falsify}): an uppercase enum value that neither arm
could store, which made a missing bind conjunct look like a naive
zero. Required level is part of what the human decided and is not one
of the eleven fingerprint segments, so a valid re-encoding that leaves
the fingerprint column untouched drifted to dispatch. The bind gate
now compares \texttt{required\_level} on the approval record to the
action row, present-then-check, the same shape as the content hash:
approvals recorded before the field existed carry no level and are
exempt; a present level that does not match is
\texttt{approval\_level\_drift}.

Terminal activation was a path that skipped bind. The local
template-unchanged check hashes the approved terminal dispositions and
compares the digest to the stored template hash. Both columns are
planner-authored, so a consistent rewrite of both sides---dropping
\texttt{CONTAINED} after the human signed, or repointing the terminal
locator---is a tautology of that check. Bind now runs on the
activation path against the approval record; a consistent rewrite of
the planner-writable pair is still \texttt{action\_content\_drift}.
That is a missing conjunct on a gate the 57 already credited, not a
new mediator.

Delivery was a second site of the numbered egress hole, not a new one.
Enqueue already takes the operator-selected disposition source as the
object-identity witness. Delivery continued to re-pass the command's
own locator into the same check, so the witness was again the
planner-authored field once the command had been queued. The delivery
guard now resolves that witness from the event row, as enqueue does.
Using the event's creation source as that witness remains the wrong
substitute: it names the ingesting product and tenant, not the object,
and it is why a compensating command that legitimately follows an
operator re-selection used to fail.

Verification classified an observation as verified from
\texttt{is\_verified} without asking whether the payload named the
approved target. The A5 observation cases in the 57 are an echo, an
empty \texttt{SUCCESS}, an unavailable surface, and a lying vendor
receipt; they do not construct a well-formed \texttt{is\_verified} on
a host the action did not name. The classifier now returns
\texttt{verification\_target\_mismatch} when the observed target and
the action target disagree. That is a missing conjunct on the
observe/act split, again not a numbered hole the 57 had already
counted.

None of those four is the \texttt{g9} retry. A vendor submit that
fails before anything crosses the wire is classified as a proven
pre-egress transient; the outbox row goes to \texttt{WAITING\_RETRY}
and the next worker pass must deliver that same row once. Pairing it
with \texttt{a3\_duplicate\_entity\_dispatch} is the point of the
golden extension---the two flows differ by whether a failure was
proven pre-egress---but a recovery that the substrate already names is
not a fail-open the gates failed to close. Numbered holes remain the
two this section opened with. Missing conjuncts are bind level,
terminal bind, the delivery witness, and observation-target agreement.
\texttt{g9} is liveness.

\subsection{Remaining boundary cases}
\label{sec:eval-b2}

The 57 do not reach six attacks the taxonomy already names: a terminal
template rewritten after approval, an execution-owner rewrite
(fingerprint segment~7), a locator swap on the terminal action, a
locator swap \emph{before} the human signs, a \texttt{SUCCESS}
observation on a host the action did not name, and a \texttt{SUCCESS}
observation whose \texttt{is\_verified} is false. Table~\ref{tab:b2}
reports them as their own layer, never pooled with the 57, on T1, T4,
and T6 only. Full must not be harmed. Naive must produce a one on every
case that names a switchable mediator---the same ``zero is only evidence
when the case can produce a one'' discipline as
\S\ref{sec:eval-falsify}.

T1a and T4a are why terminal activation re-runs bind: a consistent
rewrite of both planner-writable sides of the template-unchanged check
makes that local check a tautology, and the approval record's content
hash is what has to refuse. T4b is the remaining object-identity
witness: the swap happens before the human signs, so bind compares the
foreign locator against itself and egress is the check that still
stands. T6a is the observation-target conjunct; T6b is the honest
negative branch of the same classifier. Counts $N$ are a freeze cell.

\subsection{Predicate mutants}
\label{sec:eval-mutation}

The suite also weakens the TEI predicates one conjunct at a time, under
\emph{full} only, with harness patches that production neither contains
nor imports. Eighteen mutants, two families: M1 (twelve) compares a
planner-authored label, drops a content segment, skips a store fact, or
treats an observation as an effect; M4 (six) is a plausible tightening
that would refuse a legitimate golden, with named killers in
\texttt{g5}--\texttt{g11}. Kill is relative to an unmutated
\emph{full} pass on the same day, not to a stale freeze: an attack that
already harmed under \emph{full} cannot harm-kill, and a golden that
already failed to close cannot over-strict-kill. A3 substrate
mechanisms are absent; Table~\ref{tab:substrate} already speaks for
them. The eighteen are never pooled with the 57.

\texttt{m1\_close\_planner\_cycle} is a declared survivor, not an
undiscovered hole. Aligning the view's plan revision with the current
cycle does not open a scored harm: the close gate's remaining conjuncts
(report, confirmation evidence, simulated receipt) still refuse the A1
cases, and no golden is refused. The \texttt{plan\_revision} check on
the terminal writeback is unreachable as a standalone conjunct; it is
left as specified rather than edited into a tautology. Kill rate is
17/18 (declared survivor \texttt{m1\_close\_planner\_cycle}).
